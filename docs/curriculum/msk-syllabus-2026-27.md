# Year 4 Musculoskeletal Syllabus 2026/27

> Extracted from *MSK Syllabus d2.docx*. The wording follows the source document. The bracketed codes (for example `[LO3]`, `[EM-SEPTIC]`) were added for the app: every clinical case lists the codes it covers, and `js/data/curriculum.js` holds the same codes and labels. Case coverage is in [§6](#6-case-coverage-map).

The MSK course integrates orthopaedics and rheumatology. Patients with these disorders will be seen in both primary care and secondary care settings. You should learn to assess common musculoskeletal presentations, recognise urgent threats, propose safe initial management and explain care in terms of the patient's function and goals.

The syllabus uses the 2026 GMC MLA framework and selected NICE guidance as reference points.

You should revise basic anatomy, physiology, pathology and introductory clinical skills covered in years one and two.

---

## 1. Course learning outcomes

By the end of the module you should be able to:

| Code | Outcome |
|---|---|
| `LO1` | Take a focused history covering pain, stiffness, swelling, injury mechanism, systemic symptoms, medicines, function, work and psychosocial context. |
| `LO2` | Perform a GALS screen and focused regional examination; identify synovitis, deformity, gait disturbance and neurovascular compromise. |
| `LO3` | Build a prioritised differential for acute monoarthritis, polyarthritis, regional pain, back pain, trauma and limp. |
| `LO4` | Recognise emergencies, seek senior help promptly and communicate an initial assessment using SBAR. |
| `LO5` | Select and interpret relevant blood tests, synovial fluid results and basic imaging; explain limitations and avoid unnecessary testing. |
| `LO6` | Propose evidence-informed initial care, referral and follow-up, incorporating analgesic safety, rehabilitation and shared decisions. |
| `LO7` | Explain common diagnoses, uncertainty, treatment risks and specific safety-netting advice in accessible language. |
| `LO8` | Demonstrate consent, dignity, equitable assessment, safeguarding awareness and safe handover. |

## 2. Core knowledge and clinical presentations

For each core condition, you should be able to explain the mechanism and typical presentation, identify important alternatives and red flags, justify investigations, outline initial management and referral, and discuss functional impact and prognosis.

### Emergencies and diagnostic reasoning

You must be able to recognise the following threats and explain immediate escalation. Simulation should assess prioritisation, communication and action within the student's competence.

| Code | Clinical threat | Required learning |
|---|---|---|
| `EM-SEPTIC` | Septic arthritis and deep infection | Recognise an acutely painful restricted joint, including without fever. Arrange urgent senior assessment; understand aspiration, cultures and antimicrobial pathways. Crystals do not exclude infection. |
| `EM-CES` | Cauda equina or cord compression | Elicit new bladder, bowel or sexual dysfunction, saddle sensory change and neurological deficits. Escalate urgently through the emergency spinal pathway; recognise the need for urgent imaging. |
| `EM-COMPART` | Compartment syndrome and threatened limb | Recognise escalating pain, pain on passive stretch and evolving neurovascular findings. Normal pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome. Seek immediate surgical review. |
| `EM-OPENFX` | Open fracture and major injury | Use ABCDE, identify wounds and neurovascular compromise, minimise movement and escalate. Explain immobilisation, antibiotics, tetanus assessment and specialist management principles. |
| `EM-GCA` | Giant cell arteritis | Identify new headache, jaw claudication and visual symptoms, especially with PMR. Recognise threatened vision and the need for immediate senior-led treatment and referral. |
| `EM-MALIG` | Malignancy and unstable spinal disease | Recognise concerning persistent pain, cancer history, systemic features and progressive neurological symptoms. Use urgent local cancer or spinal pathways. |

### Investigations and interpretation

| Code | Required learning |
|---|---|
| `IX-BLOODS` | Use FBC, CRP or ESR, renal and liver function, calcium and relevant targeted tests in clinical context. Normal inflammatory markers do not rule out every serious condition. |
| `IX-SEROLOGY` | Explain the role and limitations of rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP, ANA, HLA-B27 and serum urate. Avoid indiscriminate autoimmune panels. |
| `IX-SYNOVIAL` | Interpret synovial fluid cell count, microscopy, Gram stain and culture alongside the presentation; understand sampling and antibiotic timing in the context of sepsis. |
| `IX-XRAY` | Read a plain radiograph systematically: identity and view, alignment, bone, joint spaces and soft tissue. Recognise common fractures, dislocations, degenerative change and erosions. |
| `IX-IMAGING` | Explain when ultrasound, MRI, CT and DXA can answer a clinical question. Recognise occult fracture and limitations of normal initial imaging. |

## 3. Management, communication and practical skills

### Treatment principles

| Code | Principle |
|---|---|
| `TX-OA` | **Osteoarthritis and mechanical pain:** explain therapeutic exercise, self-management, weight management where appropriate, functional support and indications for specialist opinion. |
| `TX-INFLAM` | **Inflammatory arthritis:** recognise persistent synovitis and timely rheumatology referral; explain early disease-modifying treatment, monitoring and multidisciplinary care. |
| `TX-CRYSTAL` | **Crystal arthritis:** distinguish acute flare care from prevention; explain indications and principles of urate-lowering treatment and comorbidity review. |
| `TX-FRACTURE` | **Fracture care:** understand analgesia, immobilisation, operative versus non-operative principles, complications, follow-up and return to activity. |
| `TX-BONE` | **Bone health:** assess falls and fracture risk; explain exercise, nutrition, vitamin D where indicated, bone protection and fracture liaison services. |
| `TX-PAIN` | **Persistent pain:** assess mood, sleep, disability and beliefs; develop realistic goals with physiotherapy and other appropriate services. |

### Medicines and procedural safety

| Code | Required learning |
|---|---|
| `TX-MEDS` | Compare benefits and harms of analgesics, NSAIDs, corticosteroids, conventional DMARDs, biologics and osteoporosis medicines at undergraduate level. Include renal, gastrointestinal and cardiovascular risk; interactions; gastroprotection where indicated; infection risk; monitoring; pregnancy considerations; and opioid-related harms. Use the current BNF, local formulary and senior supervision for prescribing decisions. |
| `TX-PROC` | Explain the purpose, consent, asepsis, contraindications and complications of joint aspiration and injection. Explain perioperative assessment, thromboprophylaxis principles and recognition of postoperative infection, VTE and neurovascular complications. |

### Skills students should demonstrate under observation

| Code | Skill |
|---|---|
| `SK-EXAM` | GALS screening; focused shoulder, elbow, hand and wrist, spine, hip, knee, ankle and foot examination using look, feel and move. |
| `SK-GAIT` | Gait assessment; active and passive movement; selected special tests interpreted with the history rather than used in isolation. |
| `SK-LIMB` | Documented motor, sensory and vascular examination of an injured limb, including reassessment after an intervention by the clinical team. |
| `SK-NEURO` | A focused neurological examination for spinal symptoms; examination adapted to pain, disability, consent and dignity. |
| `SK-SBAR` | A concise clinical note, problem list, investigation request and SBAR referral or handover. |

## 4. Teaching programme

### Introductory week

The module begins with a one-week series of case-based learning sessions shared with Geriatric Medicine and Dermatology. There are two MSK case-based teaching sessions, each lasting 90 minutes.

**The first session covers:**
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Spondyloarthritis (including ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis and reactive arthritis)
- Polymyalgia rheumatica and vasculitis
- Treatment of the rheumatic diseases

**The second session covers:**
- Gout and pseudogout
- Fibromyalgia and chronic pain
- Osteoporosis
- Back pain (to include osteoarthritis and spondylosis)

Before these sessions it is vital that you gain some primary knowledge of the following core conditions. It is recommended that you listen to the relevant narrated PowerPoints on QM+ and access online or written teaching materials. Flashcards and PassNotes are NOT enough.

- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Polymyalgia rheumatica
- Gout
- Fibromyalgia
- Osteoporosis
- Osteoarthritis

### Placement weeks

There is a one-week orthopaedic placement and a one-week rheumatology placement.

**Orthopaedic placement:** Aim to attend at least one standard orthopaedic clinic and one fracture clinic. Attend at least one theatre session. Attend the orthopaedic tutorials as scheduled. Write up one trauma case and one standard orthopaedic case.

**Rheumatology placement:** Aim to attend two rheumatology clinics during the week and also attend the mandatory rheumatology tutorial held each Tuesday at 1300hrs (generally in Ward 14d Seminar Room). Write up one case of rheumatoid arthritis and one case of a non-autoimmune disorder, e.g. gout, osteoporosis or fibromyalgia. Be prepared to discuss these during the tutorials.

### Rheumatology tutorial topics

| Week | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Case discussions. GALS and regional examination |
| 2 | Polyarthritis, to include RA |
| 3 | Monoarthritis |
| 4 | Myalgia |
| 5 | Back pain and osteoporosis |

### Suggested minimum learning encounters

Aim to encounter an inflammatory arthritis case, a mechanical or degenerative disorder, an acute injury, a spinal presentation, a fragility fracture or falls assessment, and a rehabilitation consultation.

### Directed study each week

Suggested six hours: two hours reviewing core conditions and guidance, two hours preparing cases, one hour of retrieval practice and one hour of reflection and targeted revision. Use a condition template covering presentation, alternatives, red flags, investigations, management, referral and patient advice.
