ANCA Immunofluorescence Patterns
Ethanol-fixed neutrophils — the substrate for ANCA IF. Click to expand.
c-ANCA (Cytoplasmic)
Diffuse, granular staining of the neutrophil cytoplasm with central interlobular accentuation. Nucleus remains unstained.
▸ click to expand
Target Antigen
PR3 (Proteinase-3)
Think of
GPA (Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis)
c-ANCA/PR3 is ~90% specific for GPA when IF pattern AND ELISA are concordant. Always confirm IF with antigen-specific testing.
p-ANCA (Perinuclear)
Staining concentrated around the nucleus. Artefact of ethanol fixation — MPO migrates to the nuclear membrane.
▸ click to expand
Target Antigen
MPO (Myeloperoxidase)
Think of
MPAEGPAPauci-immune GN
p-ANCA/MPO = hallmark of MPA. Also ~40% EGPA. NB: p-ANCA on IF can be caused by non-vasculitis antibodies (anti-lactoferrin in IBD) — always confirm with MPO ELISA.
Atypical ANCA (x-ANCA)
Neither classic c-ANCA nor p-ANCA. Flat, diffuse or mixed — typically PR3/MPO ELISA negative.
▸ click to expand
Target Antigens
Lactoferrin, BPI, elastase, cathepsin G
Think of
IBD (especially UC)PSCAutoimmune hepatitis
NOT associated with primary vasculitis. Common trap — positive ANCA in IBD/liver disease ≠ vasculitis. PR3/MPO will be negative.
⚡ Key Testing Principle
International guidelines (2017 revised consensus) now recommend antigen-specific immunoassays (PR3/MPO ELISA) as the primary screening method for AAV, rather than IF alone. IF + ELISA together gives the best sensitivity and specificity. A positive IF with negative PR3/MPO should prompt consideration of alternative diagnoses.
